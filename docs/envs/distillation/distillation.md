# Distillation Benchmark

## Gymnasium Environment for a Simulated Distillation Column Process

This environment simulates the dynamics of a distillation column process using pre-trained TensorFlow models. The models were trained on synthetic data generated by a gProms (TM) model of the distillation process applied to separate methanol and water.

## Environment Description

### Distillation Process

The benchmark environment represents a continuous binary distillation column used
to separate a liquid feed mixture of methanol and water into an methanol-enriched
top product and a water-enriched bottom product. The separation exploits the
difference in boiling temperatures of the two components: methanol, with a lower
boiling point of approximately 78°C, preferentially concentrates in the rising
vapour phase, while water, boiling at approximately 100°C, accumulates in the
descending liquid phase.

The feed stream, a mixed methanol–water liquid, enters the column at mid-point
via a feed pump. Inside the column, repeated vapour–liquid equilibrium
stages drive the progressive enrichment of methanol toward the top and water
toward the bottom. Vapour leaving the top of the column enters a reflux drum,
where it is fully condensed. The condensed liquid is then split into two
streams: one portion is returned to the top of the column as liquid reflux,
and the remainder is withdrawn as the top product — the methanol-enriched
distillate — via a top product pump. At the base of the column, accumulated
liquid flows into a reboiler, where heat is applied to partially vaporise it.
The generated vapour is reintroduced into the column to sustain the upward
vapour flow, while the remaining liquid is withdrawn as the bottom product —
the water-enriched stream. Two relevant controls, reflux ratio and reboiler heating energy rate, are modeled in the process loop and can be used to simutaneously maintain an expected product purity and output stream as part of the control task, as described in the following sections.

#### Reflux Ratio

The reflux ratio determines the fraction of the condensed overhead stream that
is returned to the column as liquid reflux, as opposed to being withdrawn as
top product. Physically, a higher reflux ratio increases the internal liquid
flow within the column, which enhances vapour–liquid contact across the trays
and improves the overall separation efficiency. The consequence is a purer
methanol fraction in the top product, at the cost of reduced distillate
throughput since a larger share of the condensed liquid is recycled rather than
withdrawn. Conversely, a lower reflux ratio increases the net top product
flowrate but at the expense of purity, as less liquid recycle means fewer
equilibrium stages are effectively utilised and more water contaminates the
distillate. The reflux ratio is therefore the primary purity lever of the
column, with its most direct influence on the observed Methanol Fraction and
Water Fraction in the top outlet stream.

#### Input Energy Rate

The input energy rate governs the quantity of heat supplied to the reboiler at
the base of the column. A higher energy rate generates a greater vapour boil-up,
which increases the upward vapour flow throughout the column and drives higher
throughput across both product streams. This raises the overall Mass Flowrate of
the top product but, if not balanced by an appropriate reflux ratio, risks
reducing separation quality as the increased vapour velocity may carry more
water upward. A lower energy rate reduces vapour generation, decreasing
throughput and potentially compromising separation if the boil-up falls below
the level required to sustain adequate vapour–liquid contact. The input energy
rate is therefore the primary throughput lever of the column, with its most
direct influence on the observed Mass Flowrate.

#### Control Challenge

The two control actions are coupled and create effects on different time scales.
Increasing the energy rate boosts vapour flow and throughput,
but purity may deteriorate unless the reflux ratio is simultaneously increased to compensate.
Increasing the reflux ratio improves purity but reduces net distillate output,
which may in turn require a higher
energy rate to maintain the desired throughput. The control challenge therefore
lies in finding and maintaining the operating point at which both the target
Mass Flowrate and the target product composition — expressed as the Objective
Mass Flowrate and Objective Water Fraction setpoints — are simultaneously
satisfied, while avoiding large and abrupt changes to either control action.

### State Space

The state space of the distillation process is defined as follows.

| Num | Observation                | Min   | Max   |
|-----|----------------------------|-------|-------|
| 0   | Reflux Ratio               | 0.0   | 1.0   |
| 1   | Input Energy Rate          | 0.0   | 1.0   |
| 2   | Mass Flowrate              | 0.0   | 1.0   |
| 3   | Water Fraction             | 0.0   | 1.0   |
| 4   | Methanol Fraction           | 0.0   | 1.0   |
| 5   | Objective Mass Flowrate    | 0.0   | 1.0   |
| 6   | Objective Water Fraction   | 0.0   | 1.0   |

### Action Space

The system is controlled by the following continuous (floating point) control actions.

| Num | Action                      | Min   | Max   |
|-----|-----------------------------|-------|-------|
| 0   | Control Reflux Ratio        | 0.0   | 1.0   |
| 1   | Control Input Energy Rate   | 0.0   | 1.0   |

### Reward Function

The reward at each step is the sum of two components:

1. Setpoint adherence – the negative absolute deviation of each controlled process variable from its objective, weighted by 1.0:

   ```
   r_setpoint = -1.0 * |Mass Flowrate - Objective Mass Flowrate|
                -1.0 * |Water Fraction - Objective Water Fraction|
   ```

2. Action regularization – a penalty on the 1-step change of each control action, weighted by 0.2:

   ```
   r_action = -0.2 * |Reflux Ratio(t) - Reflux Ratio(t-1)|
              -0.2 * |Input Energy Rate(t) - Input Energy Rate(t-1)|
   ```

The total reward is `r = r_setpoint + r_action`. The maximum achievable reward is 0, attained when all process variables match their objectives and no control action changes are applied.

### Starting State

Depending on the config option "init_method", the starting state is either drawn from a set list
of random initial states ("from_data") or always using the same initial sample provided by the
"initial_observation" option ("fixed").

### Episode End

Depending on the config field "max_episode_steps", the episode is either terminated when the
maximum step limit is reached (default: 100) or is run infinitely (None).
